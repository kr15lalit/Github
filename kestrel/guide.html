00Start here

Kestrel answers one question each evening: which stocks are worth a closer look tomorrow? It measures every NSE main-board stock (about 2,500) on trend, momentum, structure and relative strength, so you study a shortlist instead of hundreds of charts.

Work from the top down. Most stocks follow the market, and stocks in strong sectors do better than stocks in weak ones:

1MarketIs it a good time to buy at all?
2SectorsWhere is money flowing?
3StocksWhich names lead those sectors?
4ChartIs there a low-risk setup now?
Kestrel is a research tool, not a tip service. It measures what charts show; it does not predict prices or tell you what to buy. Everything here is educational, not investment advice.

01Where the data comes from

  • End-of-day prices from Yahoo Finance, refreshed three times every weekday (about 6:15 pm, 3:45 am and 9:05 am IST). The date at the top of the results tells you which session you're looking at.
  • PROVISIONAL badge: sometimes Yahoo publishes the official daily figures hours late. Kestrel then builds the day from hourly prices so you aren't left a day behind. The close can differ slightly from NSE's official close, and the day's volume is incomplete, so the volume-spike column shows "--". The next refresh replaces it automatically.
  • Universe: every NSE stock in series EQ and BE, rebuilt nightly from NSE's official list, plus a list of major US stocks (switch with IN / US). Stocks need about 60 trading days of history before they appear.
  • Market-cap buckets follow NSE index membership: LC large cap = Nifty 100, MC mid cap = Midcap 150, SC small cap = Smallcap 250, Mic micro cap = everything else.
  • Earnings dates are fetched for large, mid and small caps; micro caps show "--" in those columns.

The seven tiles at the top of the screener measure breadth: how many stocks are participating. Indices like the Nifty can rise on a few heavyweights while most stocks fall. Breadth shows what's really happening underneath.

Turn breadth into a decision
  • Green light: 200-EMA breadth above 60% and rising. Act on good setups normally.
  • Yellow light: 40–60%, or rising from low levels. Smaller positions, only the strongest stocks.
  • Red light: below 40% and falling. Mostly watch, protect capital, build watchlists for later.

03The four stages of a stock

Popularised by Stan Weinstein, the stage model says every stock cycles through four phases. Knowing which stage a stock is in is the single most useful thing you can learn from a chart: you want to own Stage 2 and avoid Stage 4.

Stage 1 · Basing

After a decline, the stock goes sideways. The long average flattens and price chops around it. Volume is often low. Watch, don't buy yet. Look for tightening ranges and the EMAs converging.

Stage 2 · Advancing

Price breaks out of the base on strong volume, then makes higher highs and higher lows above a rising long average. Pullbacks find support at the 20 and 50 EMAs. This is where the big gains happen.

Stage 3 · Topping

The advance stalls. Price swings wildly around a flattening average; high-volume down days appear. Tighten stops, take profits, don't add.

Stage 4 · Declining

Price breaks below the base and stays below a falling average; every rally fails. Avoid, however cheap it looks. Big one-day bounces here are usually traps.

Kestrel's Stage 2 score (0–7)

The Stage 2 column scores each stock on seven checks inspired by Weinstein and Mark Minervini's "trend template". Each check met adds one bar:

  1. Price is above the 50-day EMA.
  2. Price is above the 200-day EMA.
  3. The 50 EMA is above the 200 EMA (medium trend above long trend).
  4. The 200 EMA is rising (higher than 20 sessions ago).
  5. Price is at least 30% above its 52-week low (it has really moved off the bottom).
  6. Price is within 25% of its 52-week high (it's near the top of its range, not broken).
  7. RSI is above 50 (momentum is positive).
ScoreUsual meaning
6–7Confirmed Stage 2 uptrend. This is your hunting ground.
4–5Late Stage 1 turning up, or Stage 3 weakening. Check the chart: is the 200 EMA turning up or rolling over?
0–3Stage 4 or early Stage 1. Avoid for buying.

07Indicators in depth

Moving averages (EMA 10, 20, 50, 200)

A moving average smooths price to show its direction. Kestrel uses exponential moving averages (EMAs), which weight recent days more and react faster than simple averages.

  • 10 EMA: the very short-term pace. Strong momentum stocks ride it.
  • 20 EMA: the short-term trend (about one month). In healthy uptrends, pullbacks often stop near it.
  • 50 EMA: the medium trend (about a quarter). Institutions watch it; a decisive break below it is often a warning.
  • 200 EMA: the long-term trend (about a year). Above and rising = bull territory; below and falling = bear territory.

Alignment (10 > 20 > 50 > 200) shows every timeframe agreeing. Convergence (10/20/50 squeezed together) shows a pause where a big move is building. A 10×20 cross is an early sign that the short-term trend has turned up; it's more meaningful when the stock is already above its 200 EMA.

RSI: Relative Strength Index (14)

RSI measures the speed of recent gains against recent losses, on a 0–100 scale (Wilder's 14-period method).

  • Above 50: gains are outpacing losses, so momentum is positive. Stage 2 stocks mostly hold 40–50 on pullbacks.
  • 60–75: strong momentum. Leaders often live here for weeks.
  • Above 75: stretched. Not a sell signal in a strong trend, but a poor moment to start buying.
  • Below 40: weak; below 30 is deeply oversold, which in a downtrend usually just means "falling".
  • RSI vs its average (the dot): RSI rising above its own 14-period average shows momentum is turning up.
RSI is not the same as Relative Strength (RS). RSI compares a stock with its own recent past. RS rank compares it with every other stock in the market. You want both strong.

Relative Strength rank (RS 1–99)

Kestrel calculates each stock's weighted return over 3, 6 and 12 months (40% / 30% / 30%) and ranks it against all others. RS 90 means it outperformed 90% of stocks. Historically, the biggest winners usually have RS above 80 before their largest moves. A stock whose RS is rising (e.g. 45 → 60 → 72 → 85) while the market is weak is showing real buying interest.

Volume and the volume spike

Volume shows conviction. Price rising on heavy volume suggests large investors buying; falling on heavy volume suggests selling (distribution). Quiet, low-volume pullbacks inside an uptrend are healthy. Breakouts on less than average volume often fail.

Pivot points

Floor pivots are calculated from the previous bar's high (H), low (L) and close (C):

PP = (H + L + C) ÷ 3  ·  R1 = 2·PP − L  ·  S1 = 2·PP − H  ·  R2 = PP + (H − L)  ·  S2 = PP − (H − L)

Kestrel shows which zone price is in, from "Below S2" up to "Above R2". Day traders use these as intraday support and resistance; for swing traders they show short-term control (above PP = buyers in charge).

Breakouts and bases

A base is a sideways period where sellers are absorbed. Kestrel flags a breakout when the close moves above the highest close of the previous 40 sessions, and the base wasn't too deep (under 35% from top to bottom). The best breakouts come from tight bases, on volume at least 1.5x normal, in Stage 2 stocks with high RS. A breakout that falls back below the breakout level within a few days is a warning sign.

Darvas box & fractals

A Darvas box is a tight range (here, the last 20 sessions within 15%) near the 52-week high; a close above the box top is the signal. A fractal (Bill Williams) is a swing high with two lower highs on each side; a close above the most recent one shows the short-term trend resuming.

Chaikin Money Flow (CMF 20)

CMF measures where each day closes within its range, weighted by volume, over 20 sessions. Closing near the highs on big volume pushes it up. Above 0 = accumulation, below 0 = distribution. Use it to confirm, not to trigger.

Earnings reaction

How the market reacted to the last results says a lot about investor appetite. A gap up that holds (or keeps rising the next day) often starts or extends a Stage 2 move. A sharp fall on results is a red flag even if the chart looked fine before.

08Step 4 · Read the chart

Click any ticker, or search for any stock, to open its chart.

  • Candles / Line: candles show each day's open, high, low and close (green = closed higher than the previous day, red = lower).
  • Daily / Weekly and 3M / 6M / 1Y / All change the timeframe. Scroll to zoom, drag to move back in time.
  • EMA 20 / 50 / 200, Volume, RSI toggle on and off; your choices are remembered.
  • Dashed line = 52-week high. Arrow "Breakout" = the breakout day. "E" = last results day.
  • The line above the chart shows open, high, low, close, volume, EMAs and RSI for whichever day your mouse is over.
  • ‹ › or the ← → keys flip to the previous / next stock in your current results, so you can review a whole scan quickly.
A 30-second chart check
  1. Which stage? (price vs a rising or falling 50/200 EMA)
  2. Where is it now? (near support at the 20/50 EMA, or extended far above?)
  3. What did volume do on the last up-move and the last pullback?
  4. Where would you be wrong? (a level below which the idea fails, which becomes your stop)

09AI analysis

The ✨ AI analysis button under each chart shows a written read of that stock, generated each evening:

  • AI analysis (when available): summary, trend, setup, volume and momentum, key support and resistance levels, risks, and what to watch. Price levels are checked against the actual data; any the AI invents are removed.
  • Kestrel read (always available): a plain-English summary built directly from the indicators by fixed rules, so it can't make anything up.

Use it as a second pair of eyes to check your own reading, not as a decision. It describes the chart and never says buy or sell.

11Watchlists, notes & templates

  • Watchlists: three lists (rename them in the Watchlists panel, e.g. "Ready", "Setting up", "Holdings"). Add stocks with the coloured dots; Show filters the table to that list.
  • Notes: one note per stock, from the Notes column or the chart window. Record the setup, the level that would trigger action, and where the idea is wrong.
  • My filters: save any combination of scans, filters, cap, sector and sort as a named template you can reload with one click.
  • My data: everything is stored in your browser only. Use Download backup regularly, and Restore it on another device or browser.

13Daily & weekly routine

Every evening · 15 minutes

  1. Market health: green, yellow or red light? Decide how active to be tomorrow.
  2. Sectors: note the top 3–5 and any big movers in rank.
  3. Run your saved templates (filtered to top sectors when the market is mixed).
  4. Flip through results with ← → in the chart. Add the best to a watchlist and write a note with your trigger and invalidation levels.
  5. Check Weekly on your finalists: the weekly trend should agree.
  6. Check Next earn. for each finalist.

Every weekend · 30 minutes

  1. Switch to Weekly and look at breadth and sector ranks: what changed this week?
  2. Open Kestrel Eye on your watchlists: who moved into Leading, who into Weakening?
  3. Remove stocks that broke down; promote ones that set up.
  4. Review your holdings' stages. Anything in Stage 3 or 4 needs a plan.
  5. Download a backup under My data.

14Managing risk

No screen picks only winners. What protects you is how much you lose when you're wrong.

  • Decide your exit before you enter. A logical stop is just below a level that should hold if the idea is right: the 20 or 50 EMA, the breakout level, or the last swing low.
  • Size from the stop. A common rule is to risk at most 1% of your capital per idea. Example: ₹5,00,000 capital → risk ₹5,000. Entry ₹500, stop ₹470 (₹30 risk per share) → buy at most 166 shares.
  • Don't chase extended stocks. If % from 20 EMA is above 10%, the stop is far away and the risk is large; wait for a pullback or a new base.
  • Respect the market. In "red light" breadth, even good setups fail more often: trade smaller or not at all.
  • Know the events. Results days can gap through any stop.

15Common mistakes

  • Buying the day's biggest gainer. The top of the 1D% sort is often micro caps spiking inside downtrends. Always check Stage 2 and RS first.
  • Treating oversold as cheap. Low RSI in Stage 4 usually means "still falling", not "bargain".
  • Ignoring liquidity. Signals in stocks with tiny turnover are unreliable, and exits can be costly.
  • Ignoring the market. Most breakouts fail when breadth is weak.
  • Too many filters. If nothing passes, the market may simply be offering nothing. Waiting is a position too.
  • No written plan. Use notes: trigger, stop, and why. It turns hunches into a process you can review.

16FAQ

Why can't I find a stock?

Kestrel covers NSE main-board stocks (series EQ and BE). Brand-new listings appear after about 60 trading days; SME-platform and BSE-only companies aren't included.

Why does the date say PROVISIONAL?

Yahoo hadn't published the official daily figures yet, so the day was built from hourly prices. It's replaced automatically at the next refresh.

Why do micro caps show "--" for earnings?

Results dates are fetched for large, mid and small caps only, to keep the nightly update fast.

Is the data real-time?

No. It's end-of-day, refreshed three times each weekday. Kestrel tells you what to watch tomorrow, not what's happening this minute.

Where are my watchlists stored?

In your browser only. Clearing site data or switching browsers loses them, so use My data → Download backup.

Daily or weekly?

Daily for timing, weekly for the bigger picture. The best candidates look good on both.

17Glossary

A sideways period after a move, where supply is absorbed before the next leg.
Breadth
How many stocks participate in a market move.
Breakout
A close above a base's resistance level.
Distribution
Heavy selling by large investors, often seen as high-volume down days near tops.
Accumulation
Steady buying by large investors, often seen as high-volume up days and quiet pullbacks.
EMA
Exponential moving average; weights recent prices more.
Extended
Far above its moving averages; higher risk to buy.
Pullback
A temporary dip within a trend.
RS rank
Performance percentile versus all other stocks.
RSI
Momentum oscillator comparing recent gains and losses (0–100).
Stage 2
The advancing phase of a stock's cycle: price above a rising long-term average.
Support / Resistance
Price areas where buying (support) or selling (resistance) has tended to appear.
Turnover
The value of shares traded in a session.
